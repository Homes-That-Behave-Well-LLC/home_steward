# What are the major systems in this Home actually costing us?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

What are the major systems in this Home actually costing us?

## Why it matters

A utility total does not reveal how much HVAC, water heating, pool equipment, appliances, servers, irrigation or vehicle charging contributed. Some systems may have eligible direct metering while others share a circuit or have only runtime descriptions.

The household wants to understand supported contributions without a persuasive chart allocating every unmeasured unit. A major cost can support a major legitimate outcome and is not automatically the best target for reduction.

## Potential evidence

Governed system/equipment relationships; circuit and equipment metering where available; HVAC/pump/appliance runtime and modes; water-heating evidence; EV/pool/irrigation summaries; eligible bills/tariffs; documented estimate assumptions and period coverage.

Categories only, not admitted sources. Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization is independent. Household-controlled network locality and sensitivity persist; derivations inherit correction, retention, expiry, deletion and revocation. No new monitoring or shadow history.

## Important boundary

Runtime is not measured energy or cost. Shared circuits, unknown loads and overlapping allocations must stay visible; no fabricated complete partition or ranking of people.

HTBW Truth remains authoritative for governed Home Facts; retrieved utility bills are not automatically Truth. Financial/billing access is separately authorized; billing access grants neither banking access nor payment authority. Knowledge/recommendations grant no action or disclosure authority. No bill payment, utility-plan change, demand-response enrollment, procurement or autonomous HVAC, battery dispatch, EV/pool schedule, irrigation or equipment change.

Measurements, source reports, estimates and unknown remain distinct. Cost is not consumption; usage change, rate change, context change and unknown remain distinct. Show cost evidence and assumptions. Weather, occupancy/travel, billing-cycle length, tariffs, equipment/setpoint changes, season/extreme events and overlapping interventions may confound comparisons.

LOWER CONSUMPTION != BETTER HOUSEHOLD OUTCOME; LOWER COST != BETTER HOUSEHOLD OUTCOME. Insufficient evidence and no action are valid.
