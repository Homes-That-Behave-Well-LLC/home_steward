# What technology have we stopped using

Status: CANONICAL SCENARIO

Developed-content maturity only; not Owner, runtime or architecture acceptance.

> Proposed synthetic scenario; no actual abandoned capability or decommissioning decision.
> Applies the [investigation evidence rules](../../architecture/knowledge-architecture.md#evidence-and-epistemic-rules).

## 1. Household request

"What technology in the Home appears to have been abandoned?" Identify technical candidates,
not people who should be judged for using or not using technology.

## 2. Existing HTBW knowledge

Foundation declared capabilities/replacements; authoritative Decision Traces/outcomes and Truth
Historical Facts; Stewardship care/lifecycle obligations; Continuity eligible experience context.

## 3. Authorized private retrieval

Minimum eligible executions/outcomes, overrides/failures, known replacement relationships and
explicit household feedback. HA history/logs require a separate future governed source relationship;
absence of permission/retention is not absence of use.

## 4. Optional public research

Permitted support/end-of-life documentation may contextualize a known technical component.
It does not determine household value or grant removal authority.

## 5. Home Steward synthesis

Distinguish disuse evidence from missing observation, seasonal/rare safety use, independent physical
controls and deliberate backup capability. Repeated failures/overrides plus eligible feedback may
justify review. Offer only KEEP / ADAPT / REMOVE candidates: KEEP where evidenced useful; ADAPT where
fit may improve; REMOVE as a household review option, not a decommission instruction.

## 6. Provenance

Source references/dates, observation opportunities, outcome/feedback scope and missing coverage.
Separate trace decisions, Historical Facts, operational logs, usage pattern and hypothesis.
Replacement timing/disuse correlation is not causal proof or a diagnosis.

## 7. Candidate relationships

Possible replacement/disuse association and disposition are candidates, not accepted Foundation
relationships, household significance or current intent.

## 8. Household confirmation

Ask whether rare/seasonal/backup use remains valuable. Confirmation of use does not authorize
disablement, removal, policy changes or erasure.

## 9. Governed durable relationship

Foundation governs eligible capability/replacement changes; Stewardship owns care/lifecycle.
No duplicate usage/Fact history or automatic disposition state is created.

## 10. Proposed actions

Propose a KEEP / ADAPT / REMOVE review through HTBW/Operational Trust and responsible owners;
Concierge coordinates only separately authorized next steps.

## 11. Prohibited autonomous actions

No preference/policy change, integration disablement, automation removal, obligation modification,
rollback/restart, device replacement/decommissioning, booking or vendor contact without HTBW authority.

## 12. HTBW authority

Foundation capability model; Stewardship significance/care; Truth Facts; Continuity context;
Identity requester; Operational Trust permissions; Concierge Trace/explanation/orchestration.

## 13. Privacy and locality

Retrieve minimum technical use evidence without ranking people. Separately scoped log data and
local derived candidates inherit source lifecycle; no automatic indefinite raw-log memory.

## 14. Unavailable / degraded behavior

For aged-out, never-retained or unobservable use, answer "I don't know whether this was abandoned."
Preserve native capabilities and obligations rather than silently pruning them.

## 15. Acceptance expectations

Lack of evidence is not proof of abandonment; rare safety/backup use remains considered;
KEEP / ADAPT / REMOVE are candidate reviews only and no technology is disabled.

## 16. Open questions

Eligible use/feedback scope, replacement evidence, review ownership and future log/query/derived
retention controls remain open; no automatic abandonment classifier is implemented.
