# Are accepted care requirements still appropriate?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

Are accepted care requirements still appropriate?

## Why it matters

An accepted care requirement may predate a later treatment, a new enclosure or a clearer material identification. A household needs to understand why reassessment might be worth discussing without losing the authority of the existing requirement. Conflicting publications or newer advice should be explainable as evidence for review, not silently substituted for governed care.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare the basis and date of accepted care with documented Asset changes and potentially relevant professional guidance. Explain discrepancies and unresolved applicability for a qualified review; do not revise obligations, limits or accepted requirements.

## Potential evidence

Governed care requirements; acceptance context; treatment reports; enclosure documentation; professional assessments; published guidance categories.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Existing requirements remain authoritative until their responsible HTBW owner governs an explicit change.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
