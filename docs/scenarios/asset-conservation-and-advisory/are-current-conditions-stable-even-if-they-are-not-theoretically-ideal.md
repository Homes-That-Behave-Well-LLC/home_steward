# Are current conditions stable even if they are not theoretically ideal?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

Are current conditions stable even if they are not theoretically ideal?

## Why it matters

Published ideals may make a household uneasy about a room that has remained consistent for years. Abruptly pursuing an ideal can raise a different question from maintaining an established environment. The household needs to understand documented stability, the quality of the historical baseline and the Asset's particular care, rather than treating consistency as proof that conditions are acceptable.

## Existing HTBW relationship

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare current conditions and their variation with available history and governed care. Explain where generic ideals differ from Asset-specific requirements, identifying any proposed care reassessment as a question for qualified professional review.

## Potential evidence

Environmental histories; governed care requirements; material and age descriptions; treatment records; professional assessments; baseline-quality information.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Stability does not prove safety; theoretical ideals do not authorize environmental changes.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
