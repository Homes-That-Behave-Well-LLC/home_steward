# What questions should we ask?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

What questions should we ask?

## Why it matters

A household may have a broad worry about an Asset but struggle to express what remains uncertain. A focused set of questions could make a professional conversation more useful by separating condition, exposure, treatment and care-authority issues. The goal is to understand the consultation, not to steer the conservator toward an AI's preferred diagnosis or intervention.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Synthesize unresolved questions from governed care, condition history and available environmental evidence. Explain which ask for clarification of documents and which require professional examination, keeping current, cumulative and fluctuating exposure concerns distinct.

## Potential evidence

Governed care; condition reports; treatment histories; environmental histories; enclosure descriptions; existing professional advice categories.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Consultation questions do not prescribe treatment, imply a diagnosis or replace the professional's independent judgment.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
