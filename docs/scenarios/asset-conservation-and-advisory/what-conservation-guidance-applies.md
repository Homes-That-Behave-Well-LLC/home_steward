# What conservation guidance applies?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

What conservation guidance applies?

## Why it matters

A household may encounter several credible-looking conservation publications that address different materials or circumstances. Finding relevant guidance is valuable only if its scope matches the Asset and does not displace accepted care. A repaired, enclosed or composite object may not fit the simple category used in a publication, making applicability itself an important question.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare guidance scope with documented material, age, treatment, enclosure and governed requirements. Explain provenance, limitations and disagreements, distinguishing published claims from household observations and professional Asset-specific assessments.

## Potential evidence

Published guidance categories; authorship and revision information; Asset descriptions; treatment histories; care requirements; professional assessments.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Retrievable guidance is neither an admitted source nor authorization to change care or perform treatment.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
