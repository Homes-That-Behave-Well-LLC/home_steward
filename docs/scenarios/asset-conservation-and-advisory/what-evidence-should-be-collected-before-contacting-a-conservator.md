# What evidence should be collected before contacting a conservator?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

What evidence should be collected before contacting a conservator?

## Why it matters

A household preparing to contact a conservator may not know which existing documentation would make the concern understandable. Scattered images, treatment history and environmental context can obscure the question when presented without dates or limitations. Identifying relevant evidence categories and existing gaps could improve the consultation without instructing the household to handle, uncover or test a sensitive Asset.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Synthesize an explanation of available documentation and missing context for the stated concern. Distinguish existing evidence from information that a conservator may request, preserving current, accumulated and fluctuating exposure questions and baseline uncertainty.

## Potential evidence

Condition reports; dated photographs; treatment histories; Asset and enclosure descriptions; governed care; environmental histories; professional-request categories.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Evidence preparation is not a photography, sampling, handling or examination procedure.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
