# Which care instructions exist only in memory?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

Which care instructions exist only in memory?

## Why it matters

A household may rely on one person's recollection of how a sensitive object was cared for, while the origin and exact wording of that advice are unclear. Understanding this reliance could support a professional clarification before continuity is lost. Recollection remains a distinct evidence category: confidence, family tradition and repeated retelling do not make remembered care accepted or safe.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare authorized recollection with documented care and professional reports. Explain which advice lacks a traceable basis and which conflicts remain, without promoting remembered instructions into governed obligations or providing their practical application.

## Potential evidence

Voluntary recollection categories; governed care; professional correspondence categories; treatment reports; condition histories; documentation-gap descriptions.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Memory is not authoritative care or consent; caretaker identity grants no access to personal recollections.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
