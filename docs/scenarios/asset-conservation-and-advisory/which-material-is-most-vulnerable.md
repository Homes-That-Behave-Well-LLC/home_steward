# Which material is most vulnerable?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

Which material is most vulnerable?

## Why it matters

A household may ask which material is most vulnerable when several Assets share an apparent exposure. Vulnerability depends on the particular condition, construction and treatment rather than a universal hierarchy of materials. Explaining that dependence could prevent the household from assuming a robust-looking object needs no review or that monetary value should dictate conservation concern.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare documented material concerns within the stated environmental context and Asset-specific care. Explain conflicting applicability and missing composition evidence, separating current extremes, accumulated exposure and fluctuation instead of producing an overall vulnerability ranking.

## Potential evidence

Material descriptions; technical reports; treatment histories; care requirements; exposure histories; professional assessments.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Do not rank rescue priorities, declare a material universally safest or infer fragility from appearance.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
