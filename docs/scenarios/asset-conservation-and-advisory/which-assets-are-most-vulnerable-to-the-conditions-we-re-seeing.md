# Which Assets are most vulnerable to the conditions we're seeing?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

Which Assets are most vulnerable to the conditions we're seeing?

## Why it matters

When several objects share a room, a household may assume they share the same vulnerability. A textile, a bronze and a repaired panel can instead raise different concerns. Understanding those differences could make a professional discussion more focused, without converting a collection comparison into a household rescue ranking or a declaration that other Assets are safe.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts; Stewardship owns governed care and obligations. Conservation advisory never replaces HTBW Risk.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare documented materials, age, treatment, enclosures and governed care against the conditions described. Explain the evidence for each concern and distinguish present extremes from repeated fluctuation, accumulated exposure and missing history.

## Potential evidence

Asset material descriptions; treatment histories; care requirements; environmental histories; condition documentation; enclosure descriptions.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

Comparative concern is not a rescue order, professional risk judgment or finding of harmlessness.

Asset-specific governed care, informed by material, age, treatment and enclosure, outranks generic published guidance. Observations, reports and inference are not Truth; correlation is not causation. AI is not a conservator, appraiser, authenticator or emergency authority; professionals make professional judgments.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
