# What condition evidence should exist before departure?

Status: VISION — NOT YET ARCHITECTED

## Household question

What condition evidence should exist before departure?

## Why it matters

A household may want to understand what departure-condition documentation an agreement expects and how that relates to existing reports. An old photograph, a recent professional assessment and a recollection have different evidentiary limits. Explaining those differences could improve preparation for qualified review without certifying the baseline, directing an examination or assuming that sufficient documentation itself opens a Custody Period.

## Existing HTBW relationship

HTBW Foundation owns Assets and descriptive relationships; Truth owns current/historical Facts. Stewardship owns Custody Period lifecycle, Check-Out/Check-In, expected-return and due/overdue obligations, reminders, history and insurance-responsibility state, together with governed care.

## Possible Home Steward extension

Compare authorized agreement expectations and governed care with available pre-departure condition evidence. Explain timing, scope, image limitations and missing context without instructing photography, handling or condition assessment.

## Potential evidence

Departure condition reports; baseline photographs; capture-context categories; treatment histories; loan agreements; governed care; professional documentation expectations.
These are categories only, not admitted sources.

## Important boundary

No new condition certification or recorded Check-Out is inferred from document presence.

Physical return is not recorded Check-In; declared custody location is not current Truth. Missing chain legs stay missing. Insurance, ownership, caretaking and custody are distinct. No shadow lifecycle or new Counterparty/role construct is envisioned.

Retrieval, derivation and disclosure each require independent Person/source/purpose/namespace/audience authorization. Unknown is a valid result. Sensitivity, locality and derived retention/revocation/deletion constraints persist. Family membership or caretaker identity grants no access.
