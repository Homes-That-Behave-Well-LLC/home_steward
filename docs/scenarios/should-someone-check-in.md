# Should someone check in

> Aging in Place and Care Awareness: synthetic household-concern capture, not emergency triage.

## 1. Household request

"Should someone check in?" Consider a human concern, not decide medically required intervention.

## 2. Existing HTBW knowledge

Truth current/historical Home Facts, Foundation Person/relationship context, Identity requester,
Stewardship declared care and existing Explainability; responsibility does not confer access.

## 3. Authorized private retrieval

Potential separately approved Person observations, household concern, care context and recipient scope.
No automatic caregiver identity, authorization or disclosure from family/caretaker status.

## 4. Optional public research

Source documentation may clarify observations; public knowledge cannot decide this person's medical
urgency or justify private Person/care disclosure.

## 5. Home Steward synthesis

Distinguish observation, household concern, authorized caregiver, disclosure and recommendation.
State evidence/uncertainty and current human intent; a possible human check-in is not a diagnosis,
medical emergency verdict, notification authorization or promise that someone will respond.

## 6. Provenance

Separate device/Home observations and household concern, with source/Person/time/coverage.
Any personal historical comparison needs explicit periods and quality limits; inference stays labeled.

## 7. Candidate relationships

Possible concern/recipient relevance does not assign care, create authority or a durable relationship.

## 8. Household confirmation

Clarify concern and intended eligible recipient; conversational agreement is not disclosure/delivery
authority. Future review/retention is an OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION.

## 9. Governed durable relationship

OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION: no check-in/care proposal object, escalation rule or notification schema.

## 10. Proposed actions

The household may decide whether to request a separately authorized human conversation/check-in.
Automatic caregiver notification is deliberately deferred; disclosure/handoff remains an OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION.

## 11. Prohibited autonomous actions

No automatic caregiver notification/escalation, diagnosis, disease/fall prediction, medication-effect
inference, treatment, clinical monitoring, emergency decision or care/policy change.

## 12. HTBW authority

Truth current Home state/history; Stewardship care obligations; Identity context; Foundation definitions;
Operational Trust action/disclosure; Continuity where applicable; Concierge and existing Explainability/Trace.

## 13. Privacy and locality

Person-scoped concern/care stays separately governed; ingestion is not disclosure permission.
Local derivatives inherit lifecycle; a useful recommendation does not authorize exposing sensitive context.

## 14. Unavailable / degraded behavior

"I don't know whether this warrants a household check-in" is valid; no assumed notice or response.
Native HTBW/emergency paths remain independent; no LLM critical path or silent cloud fallback.

## 15. Acceptance expectations

Five distinct concepts and deliberately deferred notification captured; no medical triage, delivery
guarantee, caregiver workflow architecture or runtime acceptance.

## 16. Open questions

Human review/recipient/retention and any authorized disclosure, notification or handoff remain OPEN
ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS; no caregiver handler, escalation algorithm or service mapping.
