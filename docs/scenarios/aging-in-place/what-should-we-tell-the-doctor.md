# What should we tell the doctor

Status: CANONICAL SCENARIO

Developed-content maturity only; not Owner, runtime or architecture acceptance.

> Aging in Place and Care Awareness: synthetic evidence preparation, not clinical advice or diagnosis.

## 1. Household request

"What should we tell the doctor at the appointment?" Prepare actual observations and household questions.

## 2. Existing HTBW knowledge

Truth current/historical Home Facts, Foundation Person context, eligible Identity, Stewardship care
and existing Explainability. Medical interpretation is not Home Steward's authority.

## 3. Authorized private retrieval

Potential independently approved Person observations, device measurements, household reports/events
and care history. Clinician participation does not automatically authorize retrieval or disclosure.

## 4. Optional public research

Device terminology may clarify labels/limitations; no public diagnosis search with private Person data.
Professional statements, if separately authorized, remain attributed source statements.

## 5. Home Steward synthesis

Prepare observed changes, dates, device-reported measurements, household observations, relevant events
and questions the household wants to raise. Personal comparisons need stated periods/quality/gaps.
Prepare evidence, not a diagnosis for the household to report to a clinician.

## 6. Provenance

Distinguish wearable observation, Home observation, household report, historical comparison, inference
and unknown, with source/time/precision and any personal baseline limits.

## 7. Candidate relationships

Possible timeline/context associations remain tentative, not medical causation or accepted care records.

## 8. Household confirmation

The Person/authorized participant reviews dates, observations and questions; review does not grant
clinician-disclosure authority. Retention/confirmation mechanics remain an OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION.

## 9. Governed durable relationship

OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION: no clinical summary object, care proposal schema or medical record model.

## 10. Proposed actions

The household may take human-reviewed evidence/questions to the appointment under separate disclosure
authority. Transmission, notification and handoff remain OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS.

## 11. Prohibited autonomous actions

No diagnosis, disease/fall prediction, medication-effect inference, treatment, clinical monitoring,
emergency decision, automatic caregiver/clinician notification, disclosure or care/policy change.

## 12. HTBW authority

Truth current Home state/history; Stewardship care obligations; Identity context; Foundation definitions;
Operational Trust action/disclosure; Continuity where applicable; Concierge and existing Explainability/Trace.

## 13. Privacy and locality

Person-scoped medical-adjacent evidence requires separate ingestion and disclosure permissions.
Local derivations inherit sensitivity/lifecycle; an appointment does not authorize public/shared exposure.

## 14. Unavailable / degraded behavior

"I don't know" or an explicitly incomplete summary is valid; missing evidence is not a clinical verdict.
Native HTBW/emergency mechanisms remain independent; no LLM critical path or cloud fallback.

## 15. Acceptance expectations

Six evidence-preparation outputs and human review captured; no diagnosis/treatment advice, clinical
system, transmission architecture or runtime acceptance.

## 16. Open questions

Source/summary eligibility, retention, human review and any authorized transmission/handoff remain
OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS; no clinical or communication handler.
