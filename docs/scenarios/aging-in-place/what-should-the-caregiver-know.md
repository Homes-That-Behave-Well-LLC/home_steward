# What should the caregiver know

Status: CANONICAL SCENARIO

Developed-content maturity only; not Owner, runtime or architecture acceptance.

> Aging in Place and Care Awareness: synthetic, person-scoped, non-diagnostic experience capture.

## 1. Household request

"What should the caregiver know?" Prepare appropriately limited observations for a specifically
authorized recipient, not automatic disclosure to anyone described as a caregiver.

## 2. Existing HTBW knowledge

Foundation Person/relationship definitions, Identity eligible requester context, Truth current/
historical Facts, Stewardship care obligations and existing Explainability.

## 3. Authorized private retrieval

Potential separately authorized Person care/activity/device/history and household observations.
A caretaker assignment records accountability, not access authority; existing grants remain controlling.

## 4. Optional public research

Device terminology may be relevant; public material does not create caregiver identity or authority.
No Person/care information is sent to public queries.

## 5. Home Steward synthesis

Capture Person authorization, authorized caregiver identity, disclosure scope, minimum necessary
evidence summary and information that must not be disclosed. Distinguish source reports, comparisons,
inference and unknown; do not turn household concern into a clinical conclusion.

## 6. Provenance

Sources/Person/time/quality, authorization/audience context and any selected personal comparison
period remain explicit. Wearable reports and household narratives are not interchangeable.

## 7. Candidate relationships

Potential recipient/relevance matches do not assign a caregiver, create a grant or durable care relationship.

## 8. Household confirmation

Clarify intended recipient/purpose and scope; naming the caregiver does not grant disclosure.
Future confirmation/retention is an OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION.

## 9. Governed durable relationship

OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION for any new requirement; no caregiver/grant/proposal object or schema.
Existing Stewardship/authorization records remain unchanged.

## 10. Proposed actions

The household may review a summary and separately request authorized disclosure.
Transmission, notification, caregiver workflow and handoff remain OPEN ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS.

## 11. Prohibited autonomous actions

No automatic caregiver notification/disclosure/grant, diagnosis, disease/fall prediction, medication-effect
inference, treatment, clinical monitoring, emergency decision or care/policy alteration.

## 12. HTBW authority

Truth current Home state/history; Stewardship care obligations; Identity context; Foundation definitions;
Operational Trust action/disclosure; Continuity where applicable; Concierge and existing Explainability/Trace.

## 13. Privacy and locality

Person-scoped care stays separately governed. Ingestion permission does not imply caregiver-disclosure
permission; excluded details remain excluded. Local derivatives inherit sensitivity/source lifecycle.

## 14. Unavailable / degraded behavior

Unknown audience/grant yields "I don't know what may be shared"; no protected summary is disclosed.
Native HTBW/emergency mechanisms stay independent; no LLM critical path or source/cloud fallback.

## 15. Acceptance expectations

All six disclosure considerations captured; no family/caretaker shortcut, notification/transport/
care workflow architecture, clinical finding or runtime acceptance.

## 16. Open questions

Future source/summary retention, authorized presentation and transmission/handoff remain OPEN
ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS; no caregiver handler or notification design.
